Tenor Trombone · German Boutique
Thein "Belcanto"
Modern Production · Bremen, Germany

Developed alongside soloist Peter Steiner, featuring an 8.5" cut bell with a French rim, thin 0.45mm wall, and a Hagmann open-flow valve. Highly modular and resonant.

Comparison: Bridges the gap between the compact core of standard rotary valves and the free blow of axial flows. Blends American directness with deep German color.
Player's Sound Profile

Singing tone, beautiful resonance, secure upper register, sultry lows to projecting highs.

★★★★★
Tenor Trombone · German Boutique
Thein "American 22"
Modern Production · Bremen, Germany

Designed with Nathan Zgonc (Atlanta Symphony). Features a Thayer valve, reversed tuning slide, and an 8.5" French rim yellow brass bell.

Comparison: Broader and more "American Orchestral" sounding than the Belcanto. Designed specifically to excel in massive American symphony sections.
Player's Sound Profile

Massive ceiling, broad open sound, won't break up at extreme dynamics (fff).

★★★★★
Custom Tenor Trombone · Modern Boutique
Stephens Tenor (Rotary)
Modern Production · Vermont, USA

Built by Steve Shires in his personal Vermont shop. Features his newly designed, highly airtight in-house rotary valve and a choice of Mt. Vernon or 1920s Conn style bells.

Comparison: More focused and traditional response compared to an Axial flow; incredibly even slotting that rivals vintage Mt. Vernon Bachs.
Player's Sound Profile

Vintage clarity, immediate airtight response, solid core, extremely even through partials.

★★★★☆
Custom Tenor Trombone · Modern Boutique
Stephens Tenor (Axial Valve)
Modern Production · Vermont, USA

A custom build by Steve Shires featuring a vintage New York style yellow brass bell, an axial flow valve, a wide standard-weight slide, and a bass trombone nickel crook.

Comparison: The Axial valve and wide bass crook create a wider, freer blow than the Stephens Rotary, catering to players wanting minimum resistance.
Player's Sound Profile

Broad, free-blowing, massive breadth of sound, highly flexible.

★★★★★
Custom Bass Trombone · Modern Boutique
Stephens Brass Bass
Modern Production · Vermont, USA

Steve Shires' flagship bass trombone, utilizing his proprietary inline F/Gb/D rotary valves and available with .562" or dual bore slides and heavy/light bell options.

Comparison: Highly sought-after in the pro circuit for solving the unevenness found in factory bass trombones; acts as an anchor for the whole section.
Player's Sound Profile

Deep, foundational, powerful, incredibly fast and reliable valve action.

★★★★★
Tenor Trombone · Custom Vintage
Bach 42B "Kyle Price" Custom
1964 Bell / Modern Rebuild

A "Franken-horn" combining a 1964 Mt. Vernon yellow bell, an Instrument Innovations valve block, and an Edwards outer slide with a 1960 Mt. Vernon yellow crook.

Comparison: Takes the legendary 1964 Mt. Vernon bell and fixes its ergonomic/valve shortcomings with elite modern components.
Player's Sound Profile

Meaty, colorful, classic Bach core, perfectly tension-free blow.

★★★★★
Tenor Trombone · Vintage American
Bach 42G (Corporation Era)
1970s · Elkhart, Indiana

A highly coveted 1970s Corporation-era Bach featuring an 8.5" Gold Brass bell, closed wrap F-attachment, and a lightweight (LT) slide.

Comparison: The gold brass provides immense warmth compared to yellow brass, while the LT slide counteracts the heaviness, offering crisp articulations absent in modern factory models.
Player's Sound Profile

Warm, dark sizzle, robust core that resonates beautifully at high dynamics.

★★★★☆
Tenor Trombone · Vintage American
Bach 42B (Close Wrap)
Mid-Century Mt. Vernon Era

The quintessential traditional Bach 42B. Features the standard closed-wrap F-attachment design that defined orchestral trombone sections for decades.

Comparison: While open wraps (like the 42BO) have become popular for evenness, the closed wrap provides back-pressure that many players prefer to help stabilize the lower register.
Player's Sound Profile

Classic singing core, pleasant resistance, deeply traditional orchestral tone.

★★★★☆
Custom Tenor · Historical Vintage
Bach 42B "Byron Peebles"
1965 Factory / Late 60s Mod

A highly historic horn modified by George Strucel for Byron Peebles. Features a pioneering custom open wrap, Bernard Marston valve, and a custom acid-thinned, silver-plated bell.

Comparison: Historically significant as one of the very first open-wrap and modular trombones ever made, pre-dating modern commercial open wraps.
Player's Sound Profile

Incredibly unique, highly resonant, fast narrow-slide response, legendary pedigree.

★★★★★
Tenor Trombone · Vintage American
Bach 42 (Straight Tenor)
Late 1950s · Mt. Vernon, NY

An original, unmolested Mt. Vernon Model 42 straight trombone (no F-attachment). Features the early "swoop" signature engraving and matching serial numbers.

Comparison: Without the weight and interruption of a valve section, straight 42s offer the purest, most unadulterated version of Vincent Bach's acoustic design.
Player's Sound Profile

Nimble, crystal clear, pure overtone series, authentic old-world brilliance.

★★★★★
Jazz Tenor Trombone · Artisan
Earl Williams Model 6
Mid-20th Century · Burbank, CA

Known as the "Holy Grail" of jazz trombones. Hand-built by Earl Williams in Burbank, favored by West Coast studio legends like Dick Nash and Milt Bernhart.

Comparison: Completely different animal from the large-bore orchestral horns; designed specifically for commercial/jazz lead playing with piercing agility.
Player's Sound Profile

Zippy, electric, bright lead core, unmatched upper-register agility.

★★★★★
Alto Trombone · Vintage American
Conn 35H Eb Alto
1960s · Elkhart, Indiana

An ultra-rare dual-bore Eb alto trombone featuring a "tuning-in-slide" mechanism. This design leaves the bell section completely unbroken by tuning slides.

Comparison: The tuning-in-slide provides immediate feedback and a taper that traditional tuning-in-bell altos lack. Highly prized for Baroque repertoire.
Player's Sound Profile

Sweet, lyrical, highly focused, brilliant without being harsh.

★★★★☆
